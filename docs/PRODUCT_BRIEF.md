# Digital Product — Product Vision Brief

_Status: Draft v0.4 (living document)_
_Last updated: 2026-03-03_
_Owner: Product team_

## 1) Why this exists
This brief is the single source of truth for product direction. It keeps strategy, chapter content, UX, and implementation aligned as we build a **Disciplined Entrepreneurship implementation product for one explicit ICP**.

## 2) Vision
Build the most practical execution companion for **solo founders** applying Disciplined Entrepreneurship—so one person can move from idea to validated traction with clear weekly actions.

## 3) Problem we are solving
- Solo founders consume startup frameworks but struggle to turn them into sequenced, concrete actions.
- Most alternatives are either too abstract (content-only) or too fragmented (templates without workflow).
- Solo founders lose momentum when they have to translate theory, decide priorities, and execute alone.

## 4) Who it is for (explicit ICP)
### Primary ICP (design center)
- **Solo founders** (no full cofounder/operator team), from idea to early traction, who want a structured system to make decisions and execute consistently.

### Priority demographics within the ICP
- **Older adults (typically 45+)** who want or need to supplement their income by building a practical solo business.
- **Recent grads / young adults** who have finished university but are struggling to secure a career-track role and want a structured route into entrepreneurship.

### Excluded from core ICP (for now)
- Multi-founder teams needing collaboration/admin features.
- Agency/coach-led workflows as primary use case.
- Corporate innovation teams.

## 5) Core value proposition
For **solo founders who need structure and momentum**, this product provides **a guided, chapter-by-chapter implementation system grounded in Disciplined Entrepreneurship**, by **combining concise teaching, decision-ready worksheets, and explicit next actions in one flow**, unlike **static courses, disconnected template packs, or passive book summaries**.

## 6) Product principles (decision filters)
1. **Solo-first clarity** — every step must be understandable and executable by one person.
2. **Action over analysis** — each chapter must produce a concrete output/decision.
3. **Speed to first win** — first meaningful output in first session.
4. **Trust by default** — no hype; transparent assumptions and trade-offs.
5. **Source-of-truth discipline** — source-copy parity is a hard gate before advancing chapter batches.
6. **Framework fidelity, practical UX** — preserve DE intent while reducing cognitive load.
7. **Performance is part of the product** — build for real adoption, not a fragile demo. The learner experience should stay fast and responsive as users, content, and saved learner state grow.

## 7) Scope
### In scope (current phase)
- Solo-founder onboarding path (context capture + focus setting).
- Chapter template that forces output: objective, worksheet, decision, next step.
- Initial DE chapter flow focused on customer understanding, value hypothesis, and beachhead direction.
- Progress and momentum cues designed for single-operator accountability.
- Source-copy parity workflow and release gate for chapter content.
- Baseline analytics: activation, first-output completion, 7-day return.

### Out of scope (for now)
- Team collaboration, roles, or permissions.
- Coach/advisor workspaces.
- Advanced AI automation beyond assistive guidance.
- Mobile-native app experience.
- Enterprise reporting/compliance features.

## 8) Success criteria
### User outcomes (solo-founder specific)
- First meaningful output completed in **<30 minutes** from first session.
- **≥45%** of activated solo founders complete at least one chapter output.
- **≥30%** of activated solo founders return within 7 days.

### Business outcomes
- Validated demand from solo founders for guided implementation vs static learning.
- Clear free-to-paid conversion trigger tied to implementation depth.
- Reduced rework from source-copy parity discipline.

### Product quality outcomes
- 100% source-copy parity pass on shipped chapters.
- Low confusion on “what do I do next?” in support signals.
- No critical blockers in the solo founder core path.
- Learner-facing flows remain fast and responsive as usage grows; performance is treated as an early design and implementation requirement, not a late cleanup task.

## 9) Milestones
- **Now:**
  - Lock v0.4 with solo-founder ICP + two priority demographics.
  - Finalize chapter template + parity checklist.
  - Draft first chapter batch in solo-founder language/examples.
- **Next:**
  - Ship first usable solo-founder flow to internal testers.
  - Measure activation + first-output completion + 7-day return.
  - Remove top friction points in onboarding/chapter transitions.
- **Later:**
  - Expand chapter coverage across DE sequence.
  - Add optional advisor overlays only after solo loop is proven.
  - Introduce paid tiers once retention and outcome signals are stable.

## 10) Risks & assumptions
- **Assumption:** Solo founders value guided execution more than content volume.
- **Assumption:** DE can be translated into lightweight, single-operator workflows.
- **Risk:** Over-scoping for non-ICP users dilutes focus.
  - **Mitigation:** Enforce ICP filter in backlog grooming and copy decisions.
- **Risk:** Framework language feels heavy for first-time founders.
  - **Mitigation:** Plain-language overlays + examples + user testing.
- **Risk:** Content production bottlenecks slow iteration.
  - **Mitigation:** Standardized templates, parity gate, and batch workflow.

## 11) Open questions
- What is the minimum chapter depth that creates clear willingness to pay for each priority demographic?
- Which onboarding language variants best convert older income-supplement seekers vs recent grads?
- Where should lightweight AI guidance add value without replacing user judgment?

## 12) How to use / update this brief
- Treat this as a living document and default decision anchor.
- Any roadmap item that does not improve solo-founder outcomes should be deprioritized.
- Link major scope changes back here and date-stamp revisions.
